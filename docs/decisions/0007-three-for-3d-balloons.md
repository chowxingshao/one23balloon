# 0007: Three.js for real-time 3D balloons

Status: Accepted (one23balloon client project)

The one23balloon direction calls for glossy latex, pearl and mirror-chrome balloons that recolour live when a visitor picks a palette, pop on click, and rotate as sculptures. SVG and CSS cannot render physically based reflections. Pre-rendered images cannot recolour or rotate. Lottie adds a similar weight without 3D lighting.

Use exact `three@0.186.1` (runtime) and `@types/three@0.186.0` (types). Three.js is imported only by `src/scenes/` and loaded with dynamic `import()` from `src/scripts/landing.ts`, so it stays out of the initial entry script (the measured cost of evaluating it during load is in the last paragraph). `src/lib/canvas-island/` owns the engine-agnostic lifecycle (lazy mount, off-screen pause, quality tiers, context loss, photo fallback) and is the candidate to extract into canvas-base once a second project needs 3D. Three.js itself stays out of the template.

Consequences: a lazy chunk of 144,432 bytes gzipped (579,481 bytes raw), measured on 2026-10-29 and recorded in the quality report; scenes must be disposed on navigation; Three.js releases often, so review its changelog before upgrading.

Before the 2026-10-30 follow-up (historical; superseded by the next paragraph): the chunk is kept out of the initial entry script, but the hero scene requested it immediately because the hero island was in view, so it downloaded and evaluated during initial load. The 40-41 mobile performance score comes from script evaluation during load overall (Lighthouse `mainthread-work-breakdown`: 6.3 s of script evaluation within 7.5 s of main-thread work; `bootup-time` scripting: landing script about 3.5 s, Three.js renderer about 2.5 s, GSAP about 0.4 s), together with a largest-contentful-paint delay that is driven by the script-driven hero reveal; Three.js is one part of that cost, not all of it. Lighthouse 13.5.0 (simulated throttling, 2026-10-29) scored mobile performance 40-41 on both pages against the 80 target, with 3.5 s of landing-script and 2.5 s of renderer evaluation under 4x CPU slowdown; desktop scored 72-74. Accessibility, best practices and SEO scored 100. Revisit deferring the hero scene until after the first reveal; see the quality report.

Measured cost after the 2026-10-30 performance follow-up: traces showed the load-time cost was WebGL scene construction (environment map generation and shader compilation; Lighthouse attributed it to the landing script because the build runs inside its async island loader), not chunk evaluation alone. The landing now starts the intro, the WebGL probe, the islands and the landing motion only after the load event and an idle period (`afterLoad` in `src/lib/canvas-island/lifecycle.ts`); the probe context is created only when an island needs the answer; scenes compile their materials with `compileAsync` before the first frame; and on the low tier (touch screen, four cores or fewer, or 4 GB or less) the intro is skipped and no island mounts until the visitor's first interaction (`afterInteraction`), because a hero build at load cost 1.7 s of total blocking time on Lighthouse mobile (score 65 in that variant). Lighthouse 13.5.0 now scores performance 92 (en) and 90 (zh) on mobile, where no Three.js loads during the run, and 91 (en) and 90 (zh) on desktop, where the intro and hero scene still load; the largest remaining desktop task is the intro's environment map (about 0.7 s unthrottled). The chunk is 143,887 bytes gzipped. On phones the hero scene's build cost moves to the first interaction rather than disappearing. Details are in the quality report.

Amendment (2026-11-02): the showcase redesign changes what Three.js is used for, not the decision to use it. It now powers one travelling stage (`src/scenes/stage.ts`) that holds 30 balloons on the high tier and 14 on the low tier and regroups them into named formations as the visitor scrolls, drawn in two passes (the part behind the headline plane onto a back 2D canvas, the part in front onto a front canvas) and backed by a captured poster still that stays when WebGL is missing, motion is reduced or the stage fails. Palette recolouring, click popping and the rotating sculptures described above were removed along with the palette picker, the intro and the separate hero, sculpture and closing islands. The dependency pins, the dynamic-import boundary and the `src/lib/canvas-island/` lifecycle are unchanged. The stage's measured cost is in the quality report: desktop Lighthouse performance is 71 and 72 for the redesigned page, down from the 91 and 90 recorded above for the previous landing page.
